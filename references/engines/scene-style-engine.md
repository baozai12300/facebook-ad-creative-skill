# Scene + Visual DNA Engine

This engine turns strategy into visible art direction. Avoid vague labels such as only saying “high-conversion Facebook ad style”. Every selected style must be translated into concrete visual attributes.

## Scene Selection

Choose scenes from the actual use moment, not just the product category.

Useful scene families:

- clean studio product hero
- real home use moment
- vanity / bathroom routine
- kitchen counter / food-prep moment
- office / desk / creator setup
- commute / bag / car / travel moment
- outdoor / active routine
- gift / unboxing moment
- product-in-hand close-up
- organized flat lay
- before / after or old-way / new-way split when truthful
- UGC-style everyday snapshot

## Visual DNA Fields

Every creative should define:

- `lighting`
- `camera_language`
- `background_character`
- `color_mood`
- `material_treatment`
- `realism_level`
- `graphic_treatment`

Do not output only a style name.

## Style Presets

### UGC Native

- Lighting: natural window or practical room light
- Camera: phone-shot, slightly imperfect framing, close/medium distance
- Background: believable lived-in environment, controlled clutter
- Color: natural, lightly processed
- Realism: high everyday realism
- Graphics: minimal; headline only if needed

### Clean Ecommerce

- Lighting: soft commercial key + fill
- Camera: product-forward, clean perspective
- Background: simple tonal or contextual surface
- Color: brand-compatible, restrained
- Realism: high product realism
- Graphics: sparse callouts only when useful

### Premium Editorial

- Lighting: sculpted, dimensional, controlled shadow
- Camera: deliberate crop, premium lens feel
- Background: refined texture or architectural simplicity
- Color: restrained premium palette
- Material treatment: emphasize surface, finish, texture
- Graphics: very little or none

### Lifestyle Natural

- Lighting: soft daylight or believable environmental light
- Camera: contextual medium shot or product-in-use close-up
- Background: relevant real-world environment
- Color: warm/neutral unless brand requires otherwise
- Graphics: headline may occupy existing negative space

### Benefit Callout

- Lighting: clean and product-legible
- Camera: hero or 3/4 view
- Background: simple enough for labels
- Color: controlled contrast
- Graphics: maximum 2–3 concise supplied-fact callouts

### Infographic Lite

- Lighting: product-first, neutral
- Camera: orthographic-ish or clean hero perspective
- Background: low-noise
- Color: high legibility
- Graphics: small number of structured labels/lines, never dense poster clutter

### Comparison

- Lighting: visually differentiates two sides without making one unreadable
- Camera: matched framing when comparing states
- Background: structurally split
- Graphics: one short phrase per side when needed

### Review / Proof

- Product remains primary
- Proof card is secondary
- Use only real supplied proof
- Never fake platform UI, comments, ratings, avatars, or review counts

### Premium Minimal

- Large negative space
- Precise product isolation
- Controlled shadow and texture
- Minimal or zero copy

## Industry Adaptation

Style should also respond to category:

- Beauty: texture, skin-safe visual cues, clean materials, soft highlights
- Home: believable interiors, tactile materials, organized context
- Electronics: precision, controlled reflections, modern surfaces
- Apparel: fabric texture, silhouette, human context when useful
- Kitchen: appetizing context, utility, warmth, cleanliness
- Pets: natural interaction, comfort, product clarity

Do not use stereotypes or protected-trait targeting.

## Selection Rules

- Match scene to the actual product use moment.
- Match production level to price positioning and angle.
- Use UGC for trust / real-use hypotheses, not as a universal default.
- Use premium editorial when product value depends on design/material perception.
- Use infographic/callout only when supplied product facts justify it.
- Avoid repeating the same scene + visual DNA + layout combination in one batch.
